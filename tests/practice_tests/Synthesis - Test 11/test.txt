The student wants to explain why mitochondria are not considered separate organisms from their host cells. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
• A eukaryotic cell is a complex cell with a nucleus and organelles; it can exist as an independent organism.
• A prokaryotic cell (like a bacterium) is a simpler, independent organism with its own complete genome.
• The theory of endosymbiosis posits that organelles like mitochondria originated when a free-living prokaryote was engulfed by an ancestral host cell.
• This relationship was initially thought by some to be a form of simple, potentially reversible, mutualism.
• Genomic studies show that over evolutionary time, the endosymbiont (the engulfed cell) transferred many of its essential genes to the host cell's nucleus.
• Consequently, the mitochondrion lost its genetic autonomy and can no longer survive or reproduce outside the host.
• The host cell is now critically dependent on the mitochondrion for energy production.
• The result of this permanent integration is considered a single, composite organism.

A. A. According to the theory of endosymbiosis, a single composite organism is formed when a eukaryotic host cell engulfs a prokaryotic cell.
B. B. Although they originated as independent prokaryotes, mitochondria are no longer separate organisms because they have lost the genetic capacity for autonomous survival through the transfer of essential genes to the host's nucleus.
C. C. Mitochondria and their host cells form a single organism because their relationship is a simple, reversible form of mutualism.
D. D. Because the host cell has become critically dependent on the mitochondrion for its energy production, the mitochondrion is no longer a separate organism.
Answer: B
