The constant threat posed by environmental pathogens makes a robust immune system a necessary biological feature of vertebrates. For organisms to defend against infection, the defense may be mediated by either innate immunity or adaptive immunity. Adaptive immunity is acquired and highly specific, developing only after the body encounters a particular pathogen, for example, through infection or vaccination. Potent immunological memory is a feature of this system, but this defense is only available after a primary exposure, a process that can take days or weeks to become fully effective. Moreover, the high specificity of the adaptive response means it is ineffective against novel pathogens that the body has not previously encountered. Innate immunity, by contrast, offers a broad and immediate line of defense against pathogens. Furthermore, it relies on readily available and general mechanisms, such as physical barriers and phagocytic cells, which have broad defensive capabilities.

B. a comparison of two distinct types of immune responses
C. new discoveries in the field of vaccine development
D. differences between the capabilities of physical and cellular barriers to infection
