According to the passage, the documentation of thriving ecosystems in deep-sea trenches has been aided by which of the following?

A primary reason marine biologists long assumed that deep-sea trenches were biological deserts was the observation that the seabed in these zones seemed to consist of inert sediment. The prevailing logic was that in the absence of sunlight for photosynthesis, any complex ecosystem would require a consistent "rain" of organic matter from above, which was thought to be insufficient. The formation of intricate food webs was therefore considered highly improbable under such conditions of extreme pressure and darkness. It is now clear, however, that these initial assessments were based on limited observation. Advances in remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) and deep-sea imaging systems have documented thriving ecosystems in numerous trenches. These biological hotspots are clustered around hydrothermal vents and cold seeps, featuring unique chemosynthetic organisms that derive energy from chemical reactions, many of which have been revealed as sediment layers are disturbed by tectonic activity.

A. a new theory for analyzing the chemical composition of hydrothermal vents.
B. technological advancements enabling the observation of life in extreme environments.
C. a more complete model of the effects of pressure on deep-sea sediment.
D. a decision to concentrate research efforts on areas with high rates of marine snow.
Answer: B
