Particulate matter consists of fine airborne particles. Urban areas with higher concentrations of particulate matter also experience more significant local warming, known as the heat island effect. A study by Jian Li and colleagues found that the concentration of the particles themselves cannot be the sole explanation for this intensified warming, as the observed temperature increase is much greater than that found in areas with similar particle concentrations. However, they discovered that certain volatile organic compounds (VOCs), chemicals often released from industrial sources that adhere to the particles, are released into the lower atmosphere and have a much more potent heat-trapping effect.


A. They have a much more potent effect on local warming than particulate matter alone does.
B. They are mainly composed of inert gases.
C. They are useful for reducing the amount of particulate matter in the atmosphere.
D. They can be used to track the movement of particulate matter over long distances.
Answer: A
